# Lessons 1-3 are sticking; greedy queen confirmed as the next leak

Review #2 (2026-06-22, same day as review #1) — 4 Rapid games vs ~440-470 opponents, all wins.
Rapid rating climbed 482 → 551 in one session.

## What's working (reinforce, don't re-teach)
- **Format change took immediately:** all 4 games were Rapid (600), not bullet. The Rapid/blitz/
  no-bullet decision from LR-0004 is being followed. Praise + keep holding the line.
- **Lesson 3 applied in a live game:** vs the 2.e5 line (game 4) the user played 2...Be6, 3...Bf5
  (light bishop OUT) and only then 4...e6. The exact move-order rule from Lesson 3, used correctly.
- **Conversion (Lesson 2):** as White (game 3, London) the user found a forced mate (Qf8#) rather than
  just grabbing material — punished an uncastled king cleanly.

## The leak to fix (→ Lesson 0004)
- **Greedy queen / pawn-grabbing.** Game 2: 9...Qxd4, 10...Qxb2, then Qb4+/Qb2/Qf6/Qxg5 — six queen
  moves, mostly eating pawns, while White developed with tempo (Be3, Rc1, Bd2, Ng5, O-O). The user WON
  only because the opponent then hung a knight and walked into mate. Against a non-blundering opponent the
  queen gets trapped. This is the same pattern flagged in NOTES from review #1 (e.g. ...Qxb2 vs MarianoParisi).
- Taught the "should I take it?" 3-point check (free / queen won't be chased / not behind in development)
  and the b2/b7 "poison pawn." Lesson 0004 = The Greedy Queen, built from the real game-2 position.

## Meta-observation
- Wins are currently coming from out-blundering weak opponents in tactical chaos, not from clean play.
  The blunder-check (Lesson 1) + keeping the queen home (Lesson 4) are what turn "lucky wins" into
  "safe wins." Frame future coaching around making the wins boring/reliable, not flashier.
