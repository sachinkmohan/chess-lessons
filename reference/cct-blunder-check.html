The CCT Blunder-Check

Checks · Captures · Threats — the 5-second scan before every move.

Before you touch a piece, look at the board through your opponent's eyes. Most low-rated games are decided not by clever plans but by one player handing the other a free piece. This routine catches that — both ways.

Checks

Can either side give check next move? Checks are the most forcing move in chess.

Ask: "Is my king about to be checked? Can I check theirs?"

Captures

What can be captured right now — by them, and by you?

Ask: "After my move, is any of MY pieces attacked and undefended? Did THEY leave anything I can just take?"

Threats

Quieter moves that set up a capture or check next turn (a fork, a pin, a mate threat).

Ask: "What does my opponent want to do next, and does my move allow it?"

Beginner shortcut. At ~300, master the Captures line first — it alone fixes hanging pieces and missing free material:

  1. Is anything of mine attacked and undefended? → protect or move it.
  2. Did my opponent leave anything undefended? → take it.

Do this every single move. It feels slow at first; within a few hundred games it becomes automatic — and faster than the vague worrying it replaces.
