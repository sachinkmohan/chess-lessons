Lesson 2 · Converting won games

Finishing the Job

You won the material. Now turn it into a checkmate — without stalemating.

Winning a piece is half the game. Plenty of players at your level go up a queen and still draw or lose — the clock runs out, or they accidentally stalemate the enemy king. This lesson gives you two reliable mates and the one trap that ruins them.

The golden rule of checkmating a lone king: drive it to the edge of the board. A king in the center can't be mated; a king on the rim has nowhere to run.

This already happened to you for real: vs P0RC0S you reached king + h-pawn vs king and played 72.h7 — stalemate, an instant draw from a position you were trying to win. That's the exact trap below.

Mate #1 — Two Rooks (the "ladder")

The easiest mate in chess, and nearly stalemate-proof. One rook pins the king to a rank; the other rook checks along the next rank. Then you "climb the ladder": each time the king steps toward a rook, that rook slides to the far side and keeps climbing — pushing the king to the edge, where the next check is mate.

Mate #2 — King + Queen

Powerful but dangerous. The queen herds the lone king to the edge; then you bring your own king up to support a mating check. The queen alone can never mate — she needs the king's help.

The stalemate trap. If the enemy king is not in check but has no legal move, the game is an instant draw. With a queen this is shockingly easy to do by accident. Rule of thumb: keep the queen a knight's-move away from the lone king while herding, and always leave it one escape square until your king arrives to mate.
