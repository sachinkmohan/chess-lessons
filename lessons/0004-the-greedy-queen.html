Now look what your opponent was doing on those same moves. Each time he attacked your queen, he developed a piece for free:

The cost of a queen pawn-grab. When the queen takes a pawn, the opponent usually attacks her with a developing move. You gain 1 pawn; he gains a tempo (a free move) and the initiative — and your queen risks getting trapped. A terrible trade while your own pieces sit at home.

The "should I take it?" check

Before grabbing a pawn with the queen, all three must be true:

  1. It's truly free — the pawn isn't defended, and taking it isn't a trap.
  2. Your queen won't be chased — no enemy piece can hit her with tempo right after.
  3. You're not behind in development — your own pieces are out and your king is safe.

Fail any one? Leave the pawn. The b2 / b7 pawn especially is famous bait — the "poison pawn." GM Aman Hambleton's first rule in Building Habits is literally "don't bring the queen out early."
