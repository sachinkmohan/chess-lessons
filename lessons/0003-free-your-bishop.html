Lesson 3 · Playing d5 as Black

Free Your Bishop

Built from your own games: why you lose with Black, and the one move-order rule that fixes it.

You won or drew every game as White and lost every game as Black. All four Black losses had the same root cause — and it isn't tactics or time. It's a single move played too early: …e6.

The myth that's costing you the games

You told me the plan as Black is to "build the fort: f7–e6–d5–c6–b7." Two problems.

✗ That isn't the Scandinavian

The real Scandinavian is 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 — you trade the d-pawn and develop fast. No fort. And opponents rarely play 2.exd5 anyway, so you're seldom even in it.

✓ The fort jails your own bishop

Playing …e6 blocks the diagonal your light-squared bishop (on c8) needs. It's stuck behind its own pawns all game — your worst piece.

The one rule to remember: Never play …e6 until your light-squared bishop is already outside the pawn chain — developed to f5 or g4 first. Bishop out, then e6.

Your 3-move survival plan as Black

  1. Resolve the centre. If White offers exd5, recapture. If he pushes e5 or ignores it, leave d5 and develop.
  2. Bishop out before e6, knights before the queen. Get the c8-bishop to f5/g4, play …Nf6 + …Nc6. No early queen raids.
  3. Castle. Bishop free + knight on f6 → castle kingside, connect the rooks.

Your tangible win this session: a reflex — before you ever play …e6 as Black, your bishop must already be on f5 or g4. That one habit removes the cause of all four losses we reviewed.
